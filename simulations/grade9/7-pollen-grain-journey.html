Stage 1 · Observe

Where do pollen grains come from?

Learning objectiveIdentify the parts of a flower and locate where pollen grains are produced.
Key ideaPollen grains are made inside pollen sacs in the anther. A pollen grain is not a sex cell: it is a tiny male gametophyte that makes the male sex cells (gametes).
Magnified about ×400. Pollen grains from different species, each with its own size, shape and surface pattern. Each grain has a tough outer wall that protects the living cells inside.

Stage 2 · Explore

Inside a pollen grain

Learning objectiveIdentify the exine, intine, vegetative cell and generative cell, and explain what each one does.
Key ideaA pollen grain has a two-layered wall around two living cells (three in some plants). One of these cells will make the two male gametes (sex cells).
Rotate 0°
Not every pollen grain is the same inside
Two-celled at release
Vegetative cell + generative cell. The generative cell divides later, inside the pollen tube. Most flowering plants (about 70%), e.g. lily, tomato.
Three-celled at release
Vegetative cell + two male gametes. The generative cell already divided before the pollen was released, e.g. grasses (maize, wheat), sunflower.
Stage 4 · Experiment

Pollination simulator

Learning objectiveCompare how insects and wind carry pollen, and link flower features to each method.
Key ideaPollination is the transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma. It comes before fertilisation and is not the same process.
Or drag the bee: land it on the first flower, then on the second.

What happens

  1. The bee approaches a flower, attracted by colour, scent and nectar.
  2. It lands on the flower and brushes against the anthers.
  3. Sticky, spiky pollen grains attach to its hairy body.
  4. The bee flies to another flower of the same species.
  5. Pollen grains rub off onto the sticky stigma. Pollination has happened.

Stage 3 · Understand

Self-pollination vs cross-pollination

Learning objectiveDistinguish self-pollination from cross-pollination and explain how cross-pollination increases genetic variation.
Key ideaSelf-pollination: pollen reaches a stigma on the same plant. Cross-pollination: pollen comes from a different plant of the same species.
Genetic outcome in the offspring

Stage 3 · Understand

Pollen tube growth and double fertilisation

Learning objectiveDescribe how the pollen tube carries two male gametes to the ovule, and what each gamete does there.
Key ideaPollination puts pollen on the stigma. Fertilisation happens later, inside the ovule, when male gametes fuse with female cells.
