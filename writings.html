Essays on biology, behaviour, the human body and the questions around them — plus a translation I return to often. Some are written with my students.

    Why we do what we do: the biology behind human behaviour

    Following Robert Sapolsky, we trace a single act backwards in time: from the neurons that fired a second before, through hormones, childhood and genes, to culture and evolution. Biology and psychology turn out to be two descriptions of one story.
  • Science5 November 2025

    Silent killers: the strange world of prions

    Prions carry no DNA or RNA, yet a single misfolded protein can turn healthy proteins into copies of itself and destroy the brain. A look at Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease, mad cow disease, scrapie and kuru — and why prions are so hard to kill.
  • Science2 February 2025

    Asteroid Bennu’s secrets: building blocks of life found in space

    Uncontaminated samples from asteroid Bennu, returned by NASA’s OSIRIS-REx, hold ammonia, amino acids and all five DNA and RNA bases. They do not prove life came from space, but they show its ingredients may be common across the solar system.
  • Human biology2 February 2025

    Understanding stress: a survival mechanism and a health risk

    Cortisol, adrenaline and noradrenaline prepared our ancestors to fight or flee. In short bursts the response helps us; kept switched on by modern life, it drives diabetes, heart disease and weakened immunity.
